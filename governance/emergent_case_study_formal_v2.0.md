# **NDH‑CORE / Governance**  
# **Emergent Case Study (Formal Version v2.0)**  
## **Ethical, Epistemic, Ontological & Structural Invariants**

---

## **1. Purpose of the Formal Emergent Case Study**

This document establishes the **formal governance specification** for NDH emergent case studies.  
It defines how NDH interprets:

- emergent phenomena  
- reconstruction metaphors  
- risk‑vector modeling  
- manifold‑level encoding  
- historical cases of asymmetrical power  
- ethical and epistemic boundaries  

It replaces the raw exploratory version with a **stable, enforceable set of invariants** that apply across NDH‑CORE, NDH‑PLATFORMS, and NDH‑EXPRESSIVE.

---

## **2. Definition: Emergent Case Study (NDH‑CORE)**

An **Emergent Case Study** is a governance artifact used to examine:

- structural injustice  
- epistemic uncertainty  
- representational risk  
- power asymmetries  
- historical harm  
- metaphorical reconstruction  
- systemic risk vectors  

It is **not** a clinical model, biological analysis, or diagnostic tool.  
It is a **symbolic manifold** used to understand how NDH must constrain itself.

---

## **3. Core Invariants (Mandatory)**

### **Invariant 1 — Non‑Clinical Boundary**
NDH emergent case studies must never:

- diagnose  
- predict medical outcomes  
- recommend treatments  
- operate on real biomedical data  
- simulate clinical decision support  

All NDH representations remain **symbolic**, **non‑dual**, and **non‑clinical**.

---

### **Invariant 2 — Non‑Identifiability**
NDH must not ingest, process, or model:

- identifiable patient data  
- real genomic samples  
- clinical histories  
- medical records  
- biometric signatures  

All case studies must use **public, non‑identifiable historical information** only.

---

### **Invariant 3 — Autonomy & Consent**
NDH must encode:

- autonomy  
- informed consent  
- transparency  
- benefit sharing  
- anti‑extraction principles  

Any historical case involving non‑consensual extraction (e.g., Henrietta Lacks) becomes a **boundary marker** for NDH governance.

---

### **Invariant 4 — Structural Justice**
NDH must treat emergent case studies as opportunities to:

- map systemic harm  
- identify power asymmetries  
- examine institutional loops  
- prevent exploitation  
- encode justice constraints  

Holonomy loops representing harm must be **broken**, not reinforced.

---

### **Invariant 5 — Ontological Clarity**
NDH must maintain strict distinctions:

- **substrates ≠ bodies**  
- **manifolds ≠ persons**  
- **risk vectors ≠ diagnoses**  
- **encoding ≠ identity**  
- **reconstruction metaphors ≠ biological reconstruction**  

NDH models **relations**, not **lives**.

---

### **Invariant 6 — Epistemic Humility**
NDH must explicitly acknowledge:

- the limits of modeling  
- the difference between metaphor and reality  
- the risk of over‑formalization  
- the danger of treating people as abstractions  

All emergent case studies must include epistemic disclaimers.

---

### **Invariant 7 — Ethical Primacy**
Ethical constraints override:

- technical capability  
- expressive creativity  
- architectural ambition  
- manifold complexity  

If a model risks harm, it must be redesigned or abandoned.

---

## **4. Structural Framework for Emergent Case Studies**

Every formal emergent case study must include:

### **A. Historical Context**
A concise, non‑identifiable description of the event or case.

### **B. Ethical Fault Lines**
Explicit identification of:

- exploitation  
- asymmetrical power  
- consent violations  
- structural injustice  

### **C. NDH Interpretation Layer**
Mapping the case to:

- manifold structures  
- holonomy loops  
- projection encoding  
- risk vectors  
- operator constraints  

### **D. Governance Implications**
Clear articulation of:

- what NDH must avoid  
- what NDH may model  
- what NDH must encode as invariant  
- what NDH must treat as boundary conditions  

### **E. Non‑Dual Safety Notes**
Explicit reminders that:

- NDH models systems, not bodies  
- NDH models risk, not disease  
- NDH models ethics, not medicine  

---

## **5. Example Application (Abstract, Non‑Clinical)**

### **Case Type:**  
Historical exploitation → structural injustice → governance boundary.

### **NDH Interpretation:**  
The case becomes a **holonomy warning loop**:

- extraction → institutional gain → opacity → harm → repetition  

NDH must encode operators that **break** this loop:

- transparency gates  
- consent gates  
- justice curvature  
- non‑identifiability constraints  

### **Outcome:**  
The case study becomes a **governance invariant**, not a technical model.

---

## **6. Prohibited Uses**

Formal emergent case studies must **never** be used for:

- clinical simulation  
- medical prediction  
- treatment modeling  
- biological reconstruction  
- genomic engineering  
- patient‑specific risk analysis  

NDH is a **governance and expressive architecture**, not a biomedical system.

---

## **7. Allowed Uses (Non‑Clinical, Structural)**

Formal emergent case studies may be used to:

- analyze systemic risk  
- map institutional harm loops  
- design ethical constraints  
- explore epistemic boundaries  
- examine power asymmetries  
- build governance frameworks  
- inform NDH invariants  
- support expressive artifacts  
- anchor philosophical and ontological reflection  

All uses must remain **symbolic**, **non‑clinical**, and **non‑identifiable**.
