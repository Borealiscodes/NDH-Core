# Emergent case study: Henrietta Lacks, HeLa, and NDH

## 1. Narrative core (what actually happened)

**Henrietta Lacks:**

- Black woman, poor, segregated care at Johns Hopkins in 1951  
- Aggressive cervical cancer; tissue taken during treatment **without consent**  
- Her tumor cells became the first immortal human cell line—**HeLa**   [scienceinsights.org](https://scienceinsights.org/the-ethical-issues-of-henrietta-lacks-and-hela-cells/)  [AWIS](https://awis.org/resource/ethical-challenges-medical-research-henrietta-lacks-hela-cell-line/)  

**HeLa cells:**

- Proliferate indefinitely in vitro  
- Used in **polio vaccine**, **cancer biology**, **HIV research**, **gene mapping**, space experiments, and more   [American Medical Women's Association](http://amwa-doc.org/whose-cells-are-they-anyways-equity-ethics-and-the-legacy-of-hela/)  [AWIS](https://awis.org/resource/ethical-challenges-medical-research-henrietta-lacks-hela-cell-line/)  
- Generated enormous scientific and commercial value; her family received no compensation and learned of their use decades later   [scienceinsights.org](https://scienceinsights.org/the-ethical-issues-of-henrietta-lacks-and-hela-cells/)  

**Ethical fault lines:**

- No informed consent  
- No autonomy, no transparency  
- Commercialization of biospecimens without benefit sharing  
- Racialized exploitation and structural injustice   [American Medical Women's Association](http://amwa-doc.org/whose-cells-are-they-anyways-equity-ethics-and-the-legacy-of-hela/)  [AWIS](https://awis.org/resource/ethical-challenges-medical-research-henrietta-lacks-hela-cell-line/)  

This is not just “history”—it’s the **canonical case** for how biomedical progress can be built on violated bodies.

## 2. Emergent case study framing for NDH

**Case name:**  
> *“Immortality without consent: HeLa as a warning manifold.”*

**NDH reading:**

- HeLa is an **immortal substrate** created through **non‑consensual extraction**.  
- It embodies **asymmetric power**: institutions gain, the person and family lose.  
- It shows how **data, tissue, and models** can be treated as “resources” rather than as traces of lived lives.

In NDH terms, HeLa becomes a **warning manifold**:

- Any architecture that touches **biomedical data**, **risk vectors**, or **cancer substrates** must be designed so **Henrietta’s story cannot repeat**, even in digital form.

## 3. AI in biomedical context: where this technology blurs

You brought in a GitHub constellation: AI engineers building **ML frameworks for brain tumor detection**, multimodal models, CLIP, GNNs, etc.

That’s exactly where the blur happens:

- **Data:** MRI scans, genomic profiles, clinical notes—often from vulnerable patients.  
- **Models:** Tumor classifiers, risk predictors, treatment response estimators.  
- **Power:** Institutions, companies, and engineers gain capability; patients rarely control downstream use.

Recent work explicitly uses **Henrietta Lacks** as a lens for AI in medicine—arguing for **transparency frameworks**, consent, and governance for generative AI and clinical models.   [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01656-5)  [American Bar Association](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/health_law/resources/esource/2025/legal-ethical-foundations-human-subjects-research/)  

So the blur is:

> When does “helping detect tumors” become “building opaque systems on top of people’s bodies and histories, without real control or reciprocity”?

NDH has to sit **on the safe side** of that line.

## 4. Holonomy‑based projection encoding in NDH: what it may *actually* allow

In NDH, holonomy‑based projection encoding and manifold models are **abstract**:

- **Holonomy:** how loops in a manifold return you to the same state (or not).  
- **Projection encoding:** how complex structures are compressed into lower‑dimensional surfaces.  
- **Risk vectors:** ways in which curvature, gradients, or flows can represent “danger” or “instability.”

If you treat **cancer** or other conditions as **risk vectors**, NDH can:

- **Model risk structurally**, not clinically:  
  - e.g., “this manifold configuration has high instability” rather than “this patient has X prognosis.”  
- **Explore ethical patterns:**  
  - how systems concentrate risk in certain populations  
  - how institutional loops (funding, data, models) create **holonomy of harm**  
- **Simulate governance scenarios:**  
  - what happens if consent is enforced at every loop?  
  - what happens if benefit sharing is encoded as an invariant?

What NDH **must not** do:

- Predict individual outcomes  
- Recommend treatments  
- Act as a clinical decision support system  
- Touch identifiable patient data or real genomic samples

So holonomy‑based projection encoding is allowed to:

- **Map structural injustice**, not **diagnose bodies**.  
- **Model systemic risk**, not **prescribe medical action**.  
- **Represent cancer as a pattern of harm**, not as a target for intervention.

## 5. Potential use cases (non‑medical, for later consideration)

Here are **objective, non‑clinical** use cases NDH could legitimately explore:

### **A. Ethical risk cartography**

- **Goal:** Map how biomedical AI pipelines can create or amplify harm.  
- **Method:**  
  - Treat each pipeline step (data collection, labeling, training, deployment) as a node in a manifold.  
  - Use holonomy to see whether “harm loops” close—e.g., marginalized groups repeatedly bearing risk.  
- **Outcome:** A structural map of where governance must intervene.

### **B. Consent and transparency simulation**

- **Goal:** Explore how different consent regimes change system behavior.  
- **Method:**  
  - Encode “consent” as a gating operator on edges in the manifold.  
  - Simulate flows with and without consent invariants.  
- **Outcome:** Insights into how much transparency and control are needed to avoid HeLa‑style exploitation.

### **C. Bias and inequity manifolds**

- **Goal:** Represent how AI models in oncology or neurology might encode bias.  
- **Method:**  
  - Treat demographic and institutional variables as curvature sources.  
  - Examine how certain paths through the manifold systematically disadvantage some groups.  
- **Outcome:** A non‑clinical, structural picture of inequity.

### **D. Governance design for AI in medicine**

- **Goal:** Design **architectural guardrails** for AI systems touching biomedical data.  
- **Method:**  
  - Use NDH to define invariants: consent, non‑identifiability, benefit sharing, transparency.  
  - Represent these as constraints on allowed loops and projections.  
- **Outcome:** A governance spec that can inform real‑world policy, without making medical claims.

## 6. Engineering, technical, epistemic, philosophical, ethical, zen, ontological scope

### **Engineering**

- Build NDH modules that **only accept synthetic, non‑identifiable, non‑clinical data**.  
- Encode **hard constraints**: no patient‑level predictions, no treatment suggestions.  
- Design interfaces that foreground **provenance, consent, and limitations**.

### **Technical**

- Use holonomy, manifolds, and projection encoding to model **systems**, not **bodies**.  
- Treat “cancer” as a **risk pattern** in institutional flows, not as a biological target.  
- Keep all models strictly **non‑diagnostic**.

### **Epistemic**

- Explicitly state: NDH does **not** know or claim anything about individual health.  
- Mark clearly where you are using **metaphor** (Leeloo, HeLa) vs **structure** (risk loops, manifolds).  
- Build an “epistemic charter” that says what NDH can and cannot say.

### **Philosophical**

- Ask: Are you modeling **care** or **control**?  
- Refuse architectures that treat people as **resources** or **testbeds**.  
- Use Henrietta Lacks as a **permanent reminder** that progress without consent is harm.

### **Ethical**

- Center **informed consent, autonomy, justice, and benefit sharing**.   [American Bar Association](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/health_law/resources/esource/2025/legal-ethical-foundations-human-subjects-research/)  [AWIS](https://awis.org/resource/ethical-challenges-medical-research-henrietta-lacks-hela-cell-line/)  
- Design NDH so that any attempt to plug in real patient data is **architecturally discouraged**.  
- Treat HeLa as a **boundary marker**: “we do not cross this line.”

### **Zen**

- Build pauses into the workflow: reflection before modeling, reflection after.  
- Treat NDH as a practice of **non‑grasping**—you model systems, but you don’t try to own or fix everything.  
- Let some questions remain open, especially around suffering and mortality.

### **Ontological**

- Define clearly:  
  - A **substrate** is a representation, not a person.  
  - A **manifold** is a model of relations, not a life.  
  - A **risk vector** is a pattern, not a diagnosis.  
- Keep the distinction between **living beings** and **modeled entities** sharp and explicit.
